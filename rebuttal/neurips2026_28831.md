# → Reviewer 1JgN (Rating 3, Confidence 4)

## Q1 / W1: Does the two-direction mechanism imply Zuo et al.'s adjacency pattern? Yes, immediately.

Adjacency follows in one line from Eq. (11). The Layer-2 attention output is a
mixture of two fixed directions controlled by a single scalar,

  o_i^(2) ≈ α_{i,BOS}^(2)·w_BOS + (1 − α_{i,BOS}^(2))·w_nonBOS + b_attn^(2),

and α_{i,BOS}^(2) decreases monotonically with i. Nearby positions have nearby
α, therefore nearby representations, therefore higher cosine similarity. That is
the pattern Zuo et al. report, and we are glad to state it explicitly.

The two accounts agree on this prediction but differ in what they claim. Zuo et
al. report an observed property of the representations. We give the mechanism
that produces them, step by step: which component writes the BOS anchor
(Step 1), which parameters separate the two directions and under which two
conditions (Step 2), how the mixing coefficient acquires its position dependence
(Step 3), how the output interpolates between the two directions (Step 4), and
which readout recovers position from it (Step 5).

Step 3 we can now sharpen. Dividing Eq. (10) through by exp(s_{i,0}^(2)) gives
an exact identity,

  α_{i,BOS}^(2) = 1/(1 + i·c̄_i),  c̄_i := mean_{j≤i} exp(s_{i,j}^(2) − s_{i,0}^(2)),

which holds for every input. The causal mask supplies the count i and the QK
scores supply only c̄_i.

This is not a description we imposed. The mechanism emerges under plain gradient
descent with no position supervision anywhere, in the toy transformers and, as
of this response, in 6-layer NoPE LMs trained purely on next-token prediction
(Q2). We then confirm the model uses this code by editing it inside the forward
pass and measuring the model's own loss, which a similarity measurement cannot
do.

We therefore read the two as different levels of description rather than
competing claims. We will cite and position Zuo et al. properly in Related Work
and note the adjacency corollary in Sec. 3.

## Q2 / W2: Does the mechanism arise under a pure LM objective, and is it used causally rather than merely being available to a probe?

Yes to both. We trained two 6-layer, 12-head, d=768 NoPE transformers
(LayerNorm, GPT-2 BPE, L=128) on OpenWebText with a **pure
next-token-prediction objective** and no position supervision anywhere. `lm6`
uses ordinary sequences. `lm6bos` prepends BOS (20K iters, LR 3e-4, final val
loss 3.63).

**Emergence.** The full circuit appears in both. Below, γ is the fitted exponent
of the anchor head's schedule, α_{i,BOS} ∝ i^γ, the slope of log α against
log i. The ideal 1/i schedule is γ = −1.

| | lm6 | lm6bos |
|---|---|---|
| Position probe R², blocks 0–5 | .63 / **.98** / .98 / .97 / .97 / .52 | .92 / **.99** / .98 / .98 / .97 / .36 |
| Second-layer anchor heads | 5 (γ ∈ [−1.21, −0.38]) | **1** (γ = **−0.966**) |
| Anchor BOS-attention bias | 0.8–14.7× | **18.2×** |
| Anchor non-BOS score-ratio CV | 0.07–0.19 | 0.170 |

The profile matches the theory. Position peaks immediately after the second
block, decays slowly, and is discarded at the final block. `lm6bos` concentrates
anchoring into a single head that is almost exactly the idealized head of the
theory: α ∝ i^−0.97 ≈ 1/i, score ratios stable across positions and sequences,
and 18× attention bias onto the BOS token. The theory predicted this head, it
was not fit to it.

**Causal use, in exactly the sense you ask.** We rebuild each head's
second-layer output as α·w_BOS + (1−α)·w_nonBOS + η from reference anchors and
quantify three ways: a **frozen** probe (fit on clean activations, applied
zero-shot), a **recalibrated** probe (refit on intervened activations), and the
model's **own next-token loss**. For `lm6bos`, clean MAE 3.08 and loss 3.622:

| intervention | MAE | Δ loss |
|---|---|---|
| none (clean) | 3.08 | 0 |
| η → 0 | 59.4 | **+1.28** |
| α·w_BOS + (1−α)·w_nonBOS → 0 | 40.2 | **+1.42** |
| α → position-wise mean | 3.32 | +0.007 |
| η → constant norm | 2.90 | +0.038 |
| η → position-wise mean | **1.86** | +0.117 |

Destroying the code costs an order of magnitude more than magnitude-matched
edits. The three measurements separate what is used from what is merely
available. With η deleted, a recalibrated probe still recovers much of the
position information (R² 0.91, MAE 9.1), so the information is largely still
present in other directions. But the frozen probe collapses (MAE 4.27 to 88.9 in
`lm6`, undiminished four blocks downstream) and the model's own loss rises 1.28
nats. The model does not use what a fresh probe can still find. It uses the
specific code the circuit produces.

η's deterministic component carries the position code and its sequence-specific
fluctuation does not. Deleting η and replacing it with its position-wise mean
differ only in whether that component is present, and the difference is worth
**1.16 nats** of next-token loss. Replacing η with its position-wise mean
removes every sequence-specific component, more than the constant-norm edit that
retains direction, yet position readout improves on the unmodified model, 1.86
against 3.08 MAE. A cue read off sample-specific noise would degrade under this
edit. A deterministic position-indexed code improves.

**The mechanism is also prescriptive.** Past its trained context the model's
α-schedules leave their trained power law, with γ moving from −0.97 to about
−1.9 and then −1.3 out to 16×. Re-imposing the trained law at unseen positions
is a pure attention-weight edit with **no retraining**, and it recovers
next-token loss at L=2048:

| CE per bin | trained | 128–255 | 256–511 | 512–1023 | 1024–2047 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| unmodified | 3.66 | 4.33 | 6.40 | 7.22 | 7.78 |
| schedules enforced | 3.66 | 4.26 | **6.09** | **6.98** | **7.54** |
| constant-α control | 3.66 | 4.32 | 6.38 | 7.20 | 7.75 |

The gain is **0.24–0.31 nats per unseen octave**, 21–27% of perplexity,
reproduced at L=512 (6.35 to 6.04) and L=1024 (6.37 to 6.04, 7.15 to 6.88). The
constant-α control recovers nothing, so the gain is specific to the content of
the schedules. To our knowledge this is the first case where an identified NoPE
positional mechanism has been used to improve a model's own language-modeling
loss.

We consider the depth and objective axes closed. Scale beyond about 80M
parameters remains untested.

## Limitations (your note on task specificity and architectural scope)

Limitations we will add:

- **Architectural scope.** Results cover LayerNorm decoder-only NoPE
  transformers at d=768 with at most 6 layers. RMSNorm variants, MLP-free
  architectures, and models beyond about 80M parameters are untested. The
  LayerNorm and RMSNorm distinction matters here because centering is what makes
  the population-statistics argument go through.
- **The closed-form readout is a short-context idealization.** At L ≥ 1024 the
  two directional contributions largely cancel and the one-dimensional affine
  readout of Step 5 breaks down (R² ≈ −0.06), even though position remains
  decodable by a probe.
- **Anchor dependence is a real cost.** Evaluating `lm6bos` without its
  prepended BOS collapses the second-layer probe from R² 0.986 to 0.703, while
  `lm6` is unaffected at 0.974. A guaranteed anchor buys a sharper, more
  concentrated mechanism at the price of robustness to losing it.
- **Extrapolation is bounded, and we report the negative half.** Enforcing the
  trained schedules recovers LM loss but does **not** restore the frozen probe,
  whose saturation past the trained horizon (relMAE 11, 35, 60 and 77% per
  octave) is driven by other length-fragile features. An oracle probe reads the
  same states at 9% relative error at 16× context, so the information survives
  and the loss is readout calibration. The α-schedule is therefore one necessary
  component of long-context computation rather than the whole of it.

## W1: Is the mechanism an over-formalization of trivial phenomena?

No. The softmax supplies the form of one expression and none of its content.
Position becomes decodable through a composition of four ingredients, only one
of which is architectural, and the mechanism fails if any is removed.

**(1) The causal inductive bias supplies the count.** The mask is the only place
the absolute index enters. It is what puts an i, rather than a content-dependent
quantity, into the denominator.

**(2) High-dimensional near-orthogonality supplies separability.** Under
Gaussian, and more generally sub-Gaussian, initialization, token representations
in dimension d concentrate to near-mutual orthogonality,
|cos(x_j,x_k)| = O(1/√d) (Appendix B). This is a property of the random ensemble
rather than of the architecture, and it lets a prefix average keep a recoverable
BOS component instead of drowning it in token content.

**(3) A moderate initialization scale supplies the averaging.** Only in that
regime are the Layer-1 scores small enough that the softmax is near-uniform over
the prefix, so Layer 1 computes a genuine prefix average and writes a shared
anchor into every position. Sharpen the scale and no average is taken, so the
anchor is never written.

**(4) Layer 2 learns two things that initialization does not give.** It makes
BOS salient as a key, and it stabilizes the nonBOS-to-BOS score ratios c_{i,j}
across tokens and across sequences. The second is the crux.
α_{i,BOS}^(2) = 1/(1 + i·c̄_i) is an identity in any transformer, but it is a
usable code only when c̄_i is near-constant, because that is what makes α a
function of i alone rather than of the sentence. Training is what drives that
variance down. Only then is the schedule asymptotically log-linear
(log α ≈ −log i − log c̄), and only then can LayerNorm and the MLP invert it to
read out i.

The softmax therefore contributes the algebra in (4) and nothing in (1) to (3).
None of the four steps is inevitable. Each has an identified failure mode, and
several were observed rather than hypothesized.

- **Step 1 is not automatic with depth.** A 1-layer model must decode position
  from token identity alone. It reaches R² 0.927 only after 80K steps, **4× the
  training the 2-layer model needs** to do better. The anchor is what makes the
  code cheap.
- **Step 2's separation is not guaranteed.** In the LM the second-layer heads
  are strongly heterogeneous, with per-head cos(d_BOS, d_nonBOS) ranging from
  near-antipodal to **+0.56**. Only some heads instantiate the geometry at all.
- **Step 3's schedule is a trained quantity, not a softmax identity.** `lm6`
  spreads anchoring over five heads whose exponents straddle the ideal
  (γ ∈ [−1.21, −0.38]), while `lm6bos` concentrates it into one head at
  γ = −0.966. Same architecture, same objective, same seed, but a different
  training protocol gives a materially different circuit. A softmax property
  cannot vary this way.
- **Steps 3 and 4 depend on a real anchor.** Evaluating `lm6bos` without its
  prepended BOS collapses the second-layer probe from R² 0.986 to **0.703**,
  while `lm6`, which never had a guaranteed anchor, is unaffected at 0.974. The
  interpolation is not a generic consequence of causal masking. It requires the
  anchor the model was trained with.
- **Step 5's closed form fails outside its regime.** At L ≥ 1024 the two
  directional contributions largely cancel and the affine readout gives
  R² ≈ −0.06, even though position remains decodable.

**On which step is extra novel: Step 4, and specifically the role of η.** The
submitted version treated η only as a smallness assumption, the residual that
must be negligible for the clean two-direction story to hold. The new
interventions invert that. η's deterministic position-wise mean is a
load-bearing circuit component worth **1.16 nats** of next-token loss, isolated
by the comparison above between deleting η and replacing it with that mean.
Replacing η with the mean destroys every sequence-specific component yet
improves position readout, 1.86 against 3.08 MAE. That the residual, ordinarily
dismissed as noise, carries a deterministic positional coordinate is not a
restatement of a softmax property, and the analysis found it rather than
assuming it.

To our knowledge this is the most complete deterministic account of positional
encoding in NoPE transformers. It covers the theory, every step in closed form
with the condition under which it fails, and the convergence, that gradient
descent on next-token prediction actually arrives here, in the toy transformers
and in 6-layer LMs. The prior literature establishes that a position signal is
present, by probing and localization. Presence is not mechanism. We identify the
parameters that create the signal, the single scalar that carries it, and the
components that read it, and we confirm the reading causally by editing the
circuit inside the forward pass.

Decision criteria were registered before each experiment and we report the ones
that failed. Enforcing
the α-schedules does **not** restore the frozen position probe past the trained
context, and the fixed-schedule analytic decoder fails under extrapolation
(relMAE 330% in the first unseen octave). We state both.

# → Reviewer sT2w (Rating 5, Confidence 5)

## On computational load versus the tangible benefit of explicit positional encoding

We agree the circuit is expensive relative to an explicit encoding, and we think
that is the finding rather than a drawback of it. The mechanism spends an
attention head, a directional subspace of the OV matrix, and MLP capacity on
inverting a 1/i schedule, all to produce a coordinate that a positional
embedding supplies for free. Our results now quantify what it gets in return:

- a **lower-precision** coordinate (relMAE about 10% at the second-layer
  residual, against the exactness of an explicit encoding),
- one that **degrades outside the trained range** (relMAE 11, 35, 60 and 77% per
  octave out to 16× the trained context, though ordering is preserved throughout
  at Spearman 0.98 to 0.996),
- and a readout that **stops being affine at long context** (at L ≥ 1024 the two
  directional contributions largely cancel and the one-dimensional affine
  readout gives R² ≈ −0.06).

The practical reading is therefore not that NoPE should be preferred, but that a
NoPE model spends real capacity reconstructing a degraded version of what an
encoding would have given it exactly. That is a concrete argument *for* explicit
encodings. It also supplies a mechanistic explanation for the limited length
generalization of NoPE reported by Kazemnejad et al. (2023). The positional
information is still present far past the trained horizon, since an oracle probe
reads it at 9% relative error at 16× context, but the network's own downstream
consumers were calibrated on the trained α range and misread the compressed
coordinate. We will add this discussion.

We can also make the point constructively. Re-imposing the trained α-schedules
at unseen positions is a pure attention-weight edit with no retraining, and it
recovers **0.24–0.31 nats per unseen octave**, 21–27% of perplexity, at L=2048
in a NoPE LM trained only on next-token prediction, while a constant-α control
recovers nothing. The circuit is expensive, but it is genuinely what the model
computes with.
