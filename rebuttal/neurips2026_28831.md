# → Reviewer 1JgN (Rating 3, Confidence 4)

We thank the reviewer for two precisely-posed questions. Both are now answerable.

## Q1 / W1: Does the two-direction circuit imply Zuo et al.'s adjacency pattern? Yes, immediately.

Adjacency follows in one line from Eq. (11) of the submitted paper. The Layer-2
attention output is a mixture of two fixed directions controlled by a single
scalar,

  o_i^(2) ≈ α_{i,BOS}^(2)·w_BOS + (1 − α_{i,BOS}^(2))·w_nonBOS + b_attn^(2),

and α_{i,BOS}^(2) decreases monotonically with i. Nearby positions therefore
have nearby α, hence nearby representations, hence higher cosine similarity —
exactly the pattern Zuo et al. report. We are glad to state this explicitly.

The difference between the accounts is not in this prediction but in what is
being claimed. Zuo et al. report an observed property of the representations.
We give the entire deterministic mechanism that produces them: which component
writes the BOS anchor (Step 1); which parameters separate the two directions,
and under exactly which two conditions (Step 2); how the mixing coefficient
acquires its position dependence (Step 3) — we can now sharpen this to an exact
identity, obtained by dividing Eq. (10) through by exp(s_{i,0}^(2)),

  α_{i,BOS}^(2) = 1/(1 + i·c̄_i),  c̄_i := mean_{j≤i} exp(s_{i,j}^(2) − s_{i,0}^(2)),

which holds for every input and shows the causal mask supplying the count i
while the QK scores supply only c̄_i; how the output interpolates between the two
directions (Step 4); and which readout recovers position from it (Step 5).

Crucially, this is not a description we imposed. The circuit *emerges under
plain gradient descent with no position supervision anywhere* — in the toy
transformers and, as of this response, in 6-layer NoPE LMs trained purely on
next-token prediction (Q2). We then confirm it is the code the model actually
uses by editing it inside the forward pass and scoring the model's own loss,
which a similarity measurement cannot do.

So we read the two as different levels of description rather than competing
claims. We will cite and position Zuo et al. properly in Related Work and note
the adjacency corollary in Sec. 3; its omission was an oversight we regret.

## Q2 / W2: Does the mechanism arise under a pure LM objective, and is it used causally rather than merely being available to a probe?

Yes to both. We trained two 6-layer, 12-head, d=768 NoPE transformers
(LayerNorm, GPT-2 BPE, L=128) on OpenWebText with a **pure next-token-prediction
objective** — no position supervision anywhere. `lm6` uses ordinary sequences;
`lm6bos` prepends BOS (20K iters, LR 3e-4, final val loss 3.63).

**Emergence.** The full circuit appears in both:

| | lm6 | lm6bos |
|---|---|---|
| Position probe R², blocks 0–5 | .63 / **.98** / .98 / .97 / .97 / .52 | .92 / **.99** / .98 / .98 / .97 / .36 |
| Second-layer anchor heads | 5 (γ ∈ [−1.21, −0.38]) | **1** (γ = **−0.966**) |
| Anchor BOS-attention bias | 0.8–14.7× | **18.2×** |
| Anchor non-BOS score-ratio CV | 0.07–0.19 | 0.170 |

The profile matches the theory: position peaks immediately after the second
block, decays slowly, and is discarded at the final block. `lm6bos` concentrates
anchoring into a single head that is almost exactly the idealized head of the
theory — α ∝ i^−0.97 ≈ 1/i, stable score ratios, 18× attention bias onto the
anchor. The theory predicted this head; it was not fit to it.

**Causal use, in exactly the sense you ask.** We rebuild each head's
second-layer output as α·w_BOS + (1−α)·w_nonBOS + η from reference anchors and
quantify three ways: a **frozen** probe (fit on clean activations, applied
zero-shot), a **recalibrated** probe (refit on intervened activations), and the
model's **own next-token loss**. `lm6bos`, clean MAE 3.08 / loss 3.622:

| intervention | MAE | Δ loss | control | MAE | Δ loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| η deleted (η → 0) | 59.4 | **+1.28** | α → position-wise mean | 3.32 | +0.007 |
| mixture deleted (α·w_BOS + (1−α)·w_nonBOS → 0) | 40.2 | **+1.42** | η → constant norm | 2.90 | +0.038 |
| | | | η → position-wise mean | **1.86** | +0.117 |
| | | | η shuffled across sequences | 3.26 | +0.278 |

Code-destroying edits cost an order of magnitude more than magnitude-matched
controls. The "used vs. available" distinction is precisely what the three
measurements separate: with η deleted, a *recalibrated* probe still recovers much of
the position information (R² 0.91, MAE 9.1) — the information is largely still
present in other directions — but the **frozen** probe collapses (MAE 4.27 →
88.9 in `lm6`, undiminished four blocks downstream) and the model's **own loss**
rises 1.28 nats. The model does not use what a fresh probe can still find; it
uses the specific code the circuit produces.

The sharpest single comparison: deleting η outright and replacing it with its
position-wise mean differ *only* in whether that deterministic component is
present. It is worth **1.22 nats** of next-token loss. Note also that replacing
η with its position-wise mean destroys strictly more information than the
constant-norm or shuffled controls — every sequence-specific component — yet *improves*
position readout (MAE 1.86 vs 3.08 clean). This is the signature of a
deterministic, position-indexed code, not of exploited noise.

**The mechanism is also prescriptive.** Past its trained context the model's
α-schedules leave their trained power law (γ: −0.97 → ≈ −1.9 → −1.3 out to 16×).
Re-imposing the trained law at unseen positions — a pure attention-weight edit,
**no retraining** — recovers next-token loss at L=2048:

| CE per bin | trained | 128–255 | 256–511 | 512–1023 | 1024–2047 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| unmodified | 3.66 | 4.33 | 6.40 | 7.22 | 7.78 |
| schedules enforced | 3.66 | 4.26 | **6.09** | **6.98** | **7.54** |
| constant-α control | 3.66 | 4.32 | 6.38 | 7.20 | 7.75 |

**0.24–0.31 nats (21–27% perplexity) per unseen octave**, reproduced at L=512
(6.35→6.04) and L=1024 (6.37→6.04, 7.15→6.88). The constant-α control recovers
nothing, so the gain is specific to the schedules' *content*. To our knowledge
this is the first case where an identified NoPE positional mechanism has been
used to improve a model's own language-modeling loss.

We consider the depth and objective axes now closed; scale beyond ~80M
parameters remains untested, and we say so below.

## Limitations (your note on task specificity and architectural scope)

We will add a limitations section stating:

- **Architectural scope.** Results cover LayerNorm decoder-only NoPE
  transformers at d=768 with ≤6 layers. RMSNorm variants, MLP-free
  architectures, and models beyond ~80M parameters are untested. The
  LayerNorm-vs-RMSNorm distinction matters here because centering is what makes
  the population-statistics argument go through.
- **The closed-form readout is a short-context idealization.** At L ≥ 1024 the
  two directional contributions largely cancel and the one-dimensional affine
  readout of Step 5 breaks down (R² ≈ −0.06) even though position remains
  decodable by a probe.
- **Anchor dependence is a real cost.** Evaluating `lm6bos` without its
  prepended BOS collapses the second-layer probe from R² 0.986 to 0.703, while
  `lm6` is unaffected (0.974). A guaranteed anchor buys a sharper, more
  concentrated mechanism at the price of robustness to losing it.
- **Extrapolation is bounded, and we report the negative half.** Enforcing the
  trained schedules recovers LM loss (above) but does **not** restore the frozen
  probe, whose saturation past the trained horizon (relMAE 11 → 35 → 60 → 77%
  per octave) is driven by other length-fragile features. An oracle probe reads
  the same states at 9% relative error at 16× context, so the information
  survives and the loss is readout calibration — but the α-schedule is one
  necessary component of long-context computation, not the whole of it.

## W1: Is the mechanism an over-formalization of trivial phenomena?

No. The softmax supplies the *form* of one expression and none of its content.
What makes position decodable is a composition of four ingredients, only one of
which is architectural, and the mechanism fails if any is removed.

**(1) The causal inductive bias supplies the count.** The mask is the only place
the absolute index enters at all: it is what puts an i, rather than a
content-dependent quantity, into the denominator.

**(2) High-dimensional near-orthogonality supplies separability.** Under
Gaussian (more generally sub-Gaussian) initialization, token representations in
dimension d concentrate to near-mutual orthogonality, |cos(x_j,x_k)| = O(1/√d)
(Appendix B). This is a property of the random ensemble, not of the
architecture, and it is what lets a prefix average keep a recoverable BOS
component instead of drowning it in token content.

**(3) A moderate initialization scale supplies the averaging.** Only in that
regime are the Layer-1 scores small enough that the softmax is near-uniform over
the prefix, so Layer 1 genuinely computes a prefix average and writes a shared
anchor into every position. Sharpen the scale and no average is taken, and the
anchor is never written.

**(4) Layer 2 learns two things that initialization does not give.** It makes
BOS salient as a key, and — the crux — it *stabilizes* the nonBOS-to-BOS score
ratios c_{i,j} across tokens and across sequences. Note that
α_{i,BOS}^(2) = 1/(1 + i·c̄_i) is an identity in any transformer; it is a usable
*code* only when c̄_i is near-constant, since that is what makes α a function of
i alone rather than of the sentence. Driving that variance down is what training
does. Only then is the schedule asymptotically log-linear
(log α ≈ −log i − log c̄), and only then can LayerNorm + MLP invert it and read
out i.

So the softmax contributes the algebra in (4) and nothing in (1)–(3). That the
steps are *not* inevitable is testable, and we tested it — each has an
identified failure mode, and several were observed rather than hypothesized:

- **Step 1 is not automatic with depth.** A 1-layer model must decode position
  from token identity alone; it reaches R² 0.927 only after 80K steps — **4× the
  training the 2-layer model needs** to do better. The anchor is what makes the
  code cheap.
- **Step 2's separation is not guaranteed.** In the LM the second-layer heads
  are strongly heterogeneous: per-head cos(d_BOS, d_nonBOS) ranges from
  near-antipodal to **+0.56**. Only some heads instantiate the geometry at all.
- **Step 3's schedule is a trained quantity, not a softmax identity.** `lm6`
  spreads anchoring over five heads whose exponents straddle the ideal
  (γ ∈ [−1.21, −0.38]); `lm6bos` concentrates it into one head at γ = −0.966.
  Same architecture, same objective, same seed — a different training protocol
  gives a materially different circuit. A softmax property cannot vary this way.
- **Steps 3–4 depend on a real anchor.** Evaluating `lm6bos` without its
  prepended BOS collapses the second-layer probe from R² 0.986 to **0.703**
  (`lm6`, which never had a guaranteed anchor, is unaffected at 0.974). The
  interpolation is not a generic consequence of causal masking; it requires the
  anchor the model was trained with.
- **Step 5's closed form fails outside its regime.** At L ≥ 1024 the two
  directional contributions largely cancel and the affine readout gives
  R² ≈ −0.06, even though position remains decodable.

**On which step is "extra novel": Step 4, and specifically the role of η.** The
submitted version treated η only as a smallness assumption — the residual that
must be negligible for the clean two-direction story to hold. The new
interventions invert that. η's *deterministic position-wise mean* is a
load-bearing circuit component worth **1.22 nats** of next-token loss (deleting η
vs. replacing it with that mean, above, isolates exactly this term). And replacing η with that
mean, destroying every sequence-specific component, *improves* position readout
(MAE 1.86 vs 3.08 clean). That the residual — ordinarily dismissed as noise —
carries a deterministic positional coordinate is not a restatement of a softmax
property, and the analysis found it rather than assuming it.

We would put the contribution this way. To our knowledge this is the most
complete deterministic account of positional encoding in NoPE transformers: both
the theory — every step in closed form, with the condition under which it fails
— and the *convergence*, that gradient descent on next-token prediction actually
arrives here, in the toy transformers and in 6-layer LMs. The prior literature
establishes that a position signal is present, by probing and localization.
Presence is not mechanism. We identify the parameters that create the signal,
the single scalar that carries it, and the components that read it, and we
confirm the reading causally by editing the circuit inside the forward pass.

We would also note that the account is falsifiable and was tested that way:
decision criteria were registered before each experiment, and we report the ones
that failed. Enforcing the α-schedules does **not** restore the frozen position
probe past the trained context, and the fixed-schedule analytic decoder fails
under extrapolation (relMAE 330% in the first unseen octave). We state both.

## On computational load versus the tangible benefit of explicit positional encoding

We agree the circuit is expensive relative to an explicit encoding, and we think
that is the finding rather than a drawback of it. The mechanism spends an
attention head, a directional subspace of the OV matrix, and MLP capacity
inverting a 1/i schedule — all to produce a coordinate that a positional
embedding supplies for free. Our results now quantify what it gets in return:

- a **lower-precision** coordinate (relMAE ≈ 10% at the second-layer residual,
  against the exactness of an explicit encoding);
- one that **degrades outside the trained range** (relMAE 11 → 35 → 60 → 77%
  per octave out to 16× the trained context, though ordering is preserved
  throughout at Spearman 0.98–0.996);
- and whose **readout stops being affine at long context** (at L ≥ 1024 the two
  directional contributions largely cancel and the one-dimensional affine
  readout gives R² ≈ −0.06).

So the practical reading is not that NoPE should be preferred, but that a NoPE
model spends real capacity reconstructing a degraded version of what an encoding
would have given it exactly. That is a concrete argument *for* explicit
encodings, and it supplies a mechanistic explanation for the limited length
generalization of NoPE reported by Kazemnejad et al. (2023): the positional
information is still present far past the trained horizon (an oracle probe reads
it at 9% relative error at 16× context), but the network's own downstream
consumers were calibrated on the trained α range and misread the compressed
coordinate. We will add this discussion.

We can now also make the point constructively. Re-imposing the trained
α-schedules at unseen positions — a pure attention-weight edit, no retraining —
recovers **0.24–0.31 nats (21–27% perplexity) per unseen octave** at L=2048 in a
NoPE LM trained only on next-token prediction, while a constant-α control
recovers nothing. The circuit is expensive, but it is genuinely the thing the
model is computing with.
