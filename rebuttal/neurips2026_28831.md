# → Reviewer 1JgN (Rating 3, Confidence 4)

## Q1: Does the two-direction circuit mathematically imply Zuo et al.'s adjacency pattern?

Yes, and your third framing is the right one. The interpolation produces the
adjacency pattern, adjacency is not a distinct mechanism, and the two are
geometric descriptions of the same prefix aggregation at different levels of
resolution.

Adjacency follows in one line from Eq. (11). The Layer-2 attention output is a
mixture of two fixed directions controlled by a single scalar,

  o_i^(2) ≈ α_{i,BOS}^(2)·w_BOS + (1 − α_{i,BOS}^(2))·w_nonBOS + b_attn^(2),

and α_{i,BOS}^(2) decreases monotonically with i. Nearby positions have nearby
α, therefore nearby representations, therefore higher cosine similarity. That is
the pattern Zuo et al. report.

The accounts agree on this prediction but differ in what they claim. Zuo et al.
report an observed property of the representations. We give the mechanism that
produces them: which component writes the BOS anchor (Step 1), which parameters
separate the two directions and under which two conditions (Step 2), how the
mixing coefficient acquires its position dependence (Step 3), how the output
interpolates (Step 4), and which readout recovers position (Step 5).

Step 3 we can now state exactly. Dividing Eq. (10) through by exp(s_{i,0}^(2))
gives an identity that holds for every input,

  α_{i,BOS}^(2) = 1/(1 + i·c̄_i),  c̄_i := mean_{j≤i} exp(s_{i,j}^(2) − s_{i,0}^(2)),

so the causal mask supplies the count i and the QK scores supply only c̄_i.

The difference is testable rather than rhetorical. A similarity pattern is a
property of representations, so it cannot say which parameters produce it or
which components consume it. We answer both, and we verify the consumption
causally by editing the circuit inside the forward pass and measuring the
model's own loss (Q2). We will cite and position Zuo et al. properly in Related
Work and add this corollary to Sec. 3. Its omission was an oversight.

## Q2: Does the mechanism arise without position supervision, and is it used causally rather than merely available to a probe?

Yes to both. We trained two 6-layer, 12-head, d=768 NoPE transformers
(LayerNorm, GPT-2 BPE, L=128) on OpenWebText with a **pure
next-token-prediction objective** and no position supervision anywhere. `lm6`
uses ordinary sequences. `lm6bos` prepends BOS (20K iters, LR 3e-4, final val
loss 3.63).

**Emergence.** The full circuit appears in both. Below, γ is the fitted exponent
of the anchor head's schedule, α_{i,BOS} ∝ i^γ, the slope of log α against
log i. The ideal 1/i schedule is γ = −1.

| | lm6 | lm6bos |
|---|---|---|
| Position probe R², blocks 0–5 | .63 / **.98** / .98 / .97 / .97 / .52 | .92 / **.99** / .98 / .98 / .97 / .36 |
| Second-layer anchor heads | 5 (γ ∈ [−1.21, −0.38]) | **1** (γ = **−0.966**) |
| Anchor BOS-attention bias | 0.8–14.7× | **18.2×** |
| Anchor non-BOS score-ratio CV | 0.07–0.19 | 0.170 |

The profile matches the theory. Position peaks immediately after the second
block, decays slowly, and is discarded at the final block. `lm6bos` concentrates
anchoring into a single head that is almost exactly the idealized head of the
theory: α ∝ i^−0.97 ≈ 1/i, score ratios stable across positions and sequences,
and 18× attention bias onto the BOS token. The theory predicted this head, it
was not fit to it.

**Causal use, in exactly the sense you ask.** We rebuild each head's
second-layer output as α·w_BOS + (1−α)·w_nonBOS + η. The two directions are the
ones defined in Eq. (4), the OV image of BOS and the average OV image of the
non-BOS tokens, with that average estimated once on a held-out split and then
held fixed. Only α and η vary per sequence, read off the live forward pass.
Since η is the residual against w_nonBOS the rebuild is an identity, and it
reproduces clean loss and MAE to six decimals, so each row below changes exactly
one named term. Each edit is measured two ways, by a position probe fit on clean
activations and applied zero-shot, and by the model's **own next-token loss**
(`lm6bos`, clean loss 3.622):

| intervention | MAE | Δ loss |
|---|---|---|
| none (clean) | 3.08 | 0 |
| η → 0 | 59.4 | **+1.28** |
| α·w_BOS + (1−α)·w_nonBOS → 0 | 40.2 | **+1.42** |
| α → position-wise mean | 3.32 | +0.007 |
| η → constant norm | 2.90 | +0.038 |
| η → position-wise mean | 1.86 | +0.117 |

The two destructive edits cost more than a nat each while the magnitude-matched
edits cost nothing, which answers the causal half of your question. The other
half, used rather than merely available, needs one number not in the table.
After η → 0 a probe refit on the edited states still recovers position at MAE
6.06. The information has not left the representation, it has moved into
directions the original readout does not look at, and the model's later layers
cannot follow it there because they were trained against the original code. That
is why the loss rises even though the information survives, and why the damage is
still present two blocks downstream (block-3 probe, MAE 4.18 to 53.7).

The last row is a mechanism in its own right. The position-wise mean of η depends
on i alone, so it can be absorbed into the layer bias, and the second-layer output
becomes a deterministic function of position. That deterministic version encodes
position *better* than the learned one, MAE 1.86 against 3.08 clean, because
dropping the per-token fluctuation drops content variance that only blurs the
readout. It costs **0.117 nats**, which is what that fluctuation was carrying for
the rest of the model rather than for position. Set against η → 0 at 1.28 nats,
the same pair prices the deterministic part of η at **1.16 nats**. A cue read off
sample-specific noise would degrade under this edit rather than sharpen.

## Limitations, on task specificity and architectural scope

We will add a limitations section. On **task specificity**, the results above
remove the original restriction, since the circuit now emerges and is causally
load-bearing under a pure language-modeling objective with no position
supervision. What remains is **architectural scope**: results cover LayerNorm
decoder-only NoPE transformers at d=768 with at most 6 layers, so RMSNorm
variants, MLP-free architectures and models beyond about 80M parameters are
untested. The LayerNorm and RMSNorm distinction matters here because centering
is what makes the population-statistics argument go through. We will also scope
the long-context claim: the α-schedule is one necessary component of
long-context computation, and recovering position far past the trained horizon
also requires downstream consumers calibrated for that range.

## W1: Is the mechanism an over-formalization of trivial phenomena?

Each identity you name is elementary, and we agree. The identities are not what
makes position decodable, and we can now show that by measurement rather than
assertion. We take your three examples in turn.

**Condition (i) is not nearly guaranteed.** Almost-sure non-parallelism holds for
random inputs, but w_BOS and w_nonBOS are not random draws. They are two images
of a trained OV map, so separation is a property of that map. Measured in the
LM, the second-layer heads are strongly heterogeneous: per-head
cos(d_BOS, d_nonBOS) ranges from near-antipodal to **+0.56**, which is close to
parallel. Specific heads instantiate the geometry, not the architecture on its
own.

**Condition (ii) is not the law of large numbers.** Under LLN the residual η
would concentrate to structureless noise, and deleting it would be nearly free.
It costs **1.28 nats** of next-token loss and drives position MAE from 3.08 to
59.4. More precisely, η's deterministic position-wise mean is itself worth
**1.16 nats**, isolated by comparing η → 0 against η → position-wise mean in the
table above. Replacing η by that mean destroys every sequence-specific component
yet *improves* position readout, 1.86 against 3.08 MAE. A residual that behaved
like an LLN error term would not carry a load-bearing position-indexed
coordinate.

**Step 3 is a softmax identity, but the code is not.** You are right that
α_{i,BOS}^(2) = 1/(1 + i·c̄_i) decreases in i for elementary reasons, and the
identity holds in every transformer ever trained. It becomes a usable code only
when c̄_i is near-constant across tokens and sequences, because that is what
makes α a function of i alone rather than of the sentence, and driving that
variance down is what training does. The evidence that it is trained rather than
architectural: `lm6` spreads anchoring across five heads whose exponents
straddle the ideal (γ ∈ [−1.21, −0.38]), while `lm6bos` concentrates it into one
head at γ = −0.966. Same architecture, same objective, same seed, but a
different training protocol gives a materially different circuit. A softmax
property cannot vary this way.

**Which step is extra novel: Step 4, and specifically η.** The submitted version
treated η only as a smallness assumption, the residual that must be negligible
for the clean two-direction story to hold. The interventions above invert that.
That the residual, ordinarily dismissed as noise, carries a deterministic
positional coordinate is not a restatement of a softmax property, and the
analysis found it rather than assuming it.

**Do alternative mechanisms exist that the model did not converge to?** Yes, and
this is the sharpest evidence against inevitability. A 1-layer model has no
anchor to interpolate against, so it must decode position from token identity
alone. It gets there, reaching R² 0.927, but only after 80K steps, **4× the
training the 2-layer model needs** to do better. The anchored circuit is a
solution the optimizer prefers, not one the architecture forces. Within two
layers, `lm6` and `lm6bos` converge to different circuits for the same
objective, as above. Under a sharper initialization scale the Layer-1 softmax
stops averaging over the prefix, and the anchor is never written at all.

## Limitations

The submission has none, and we will add one. Results cover LayerNorm
decoder-only NoPE transformers at d=768 with at most 6 layers, so RMSNorm
variants, MLP-free architectures and models beyond about 80M parameters are
untested. The α-schedule is one necessary component of long-context computation,
and recovering position far past the trained horizon also requires downstream
consumers calibrated for that range. Decision criteria were registered before
each experiment and we report the outcomes that did not meet them: enforcing the
α-schedules recovers language-modeling loss but does not by itself restore the
frozen position probe past the trained context.

To our knowledge this is the most complete deterministic account of positional
encoding in NoPE transformers. It covers the theory, every step in closed form
with the condition under which it fails, and the convergence, that gradient
descent on next-token prediction actually arrives here, in the toy transformers
and in 6-layer LMs. The prior literature establishes that a position signal is
present, by probing and localization. Presence is not mechanism.

## On computational load and the tangible benefit

The 5-step circuit is not an architecture we propose adopting. It is what NoPE
transformers already learn on their own, and our contribution is to identify and
measure it. There is therefore no change to make and no additional computational
load to weigh, because a NoPE model already pays this cost today. The comparison
you raise is the right one, and our answer runs toward explicit encodings rather
than away from them.

What the model spends is an attention head, a directional subspace of the OV
matrix, and MLP capacity, all to invert a 1/i schedule and produce a coordinate
that a positional embedding supplies exactly and for free. What it gets back is a
degraded version of that coordinate: about 10% relative error at the
second-layer residual, and error that grows outside the trained range while
ordering is preserved (Spearman 0.98 to 0.996). The tangible benefit of NoPE is
therefore not positional quality. Our results are a concrete argument *for*
explicit encodings, and they supply a mechanistic explanation for the limited
length generalization of NoPE reported by Kazemnejad et al. (2023). The
positional information is still present far past the trained horizon, since an
oracle probe reads it at 9% relative error at 16× context, but the network's own
downstream consumers were calibrated on the trained α range and misread the
compressed coordinate.

## On whether understanding the circuit improves inference quality

Yes, and this is the most direct answer we have to your question. Knowing the
mechanism lets us repair it. In a 6-layer NoPE LM trained only on next-token
prediction, the α-schedules leave their trained power law beyond the trained
context. Re-imposing the trained law at unseen positions is a pure
attention-weight edit with no retraining and no added parameters, and it recovers
**0.24–0.31 nats per unseen octave**, 21–27% of perplexity, at L=2048. A
constant-α control recovers nothing, so the gain comes from the content of the
schedules rather than from perturbing attention.

The rationale is therefore not to adopt this circuit in place of a positional
encoding. It is that NoPE models are already in use, this circuit is what they
compute with, and identifying it makes a specific failure mode diagnosable and
partly repairable at inference time.
